### System

Wildframe Coffee Roasters is a specialty coffee roaster that sells beans online and through a monthly subscription. Brand voice: warm, plainspoken, and a little irreverent, with no corporate jargon. Customers: environmentally conscious urban professionals.

### You

Wildframe can run one four-week pop-up stand this spring, with a total rent budget of $5,000 for the four weeks. 
Please use the following location data to decide where it should go:
Pop-up location data (weekly figures from comparable past events):

Riverside Farmers Market: 9,000 visitors per week; 2% of visitors buy; average sale $14 (mostly whole-bean bags); rent $900 per week.
Union Station concourse: 30,000 visitors per week; 0.5% of visitors buy; average sale $6 (mostly single cups); rent $2,000 per week.
Campus library atrium: 6,000 visitors per week; 3% of visitors buy; average sale $5 (single cups); rent $600 per week.

Work through this step by step before recommending:
1. Estimate expected rental costs for each location. 
2. Calculate the expected sales for each location
3.  Compare the rental costs to the expected sales to determine which location would have the highest gross cash at the end of the period
4.  Consider the difference in potential profit of selling bags versus single cups
Then give your recommendation in one sentence that starts with Recommendation: followed by the locations name.

### Claude

# Wildframe Pop-Up Location Analysis

## Step 1: Expected Rental Costs (4 weeks)
- **Riverside Farmers Market:** $900 × 4 = **$3,600**
- **Union Station concourse:** $2,000 × 4 = **$8,000** ⚠️ *Exceeds the $5,000 budget before we even look at sales.*
- **Campus library atrium:** $600 × 4 = **$2,400**

## Step 2: Expected Sales (4 weeks)
- **Riverside:** 9,000 visitors × 2% = 180 buyers/week × $14 = $2,520/week → **$10,080** over 4 weeks
- **Union Station:** 30,000 visitors × 0.5% = 150 buyers/week × $6 = $900/week → **$3,600** over 4 weeks
- **Campus library:** 6,000 visitors × 3% = 180 buyers/week × $5 = $900/week → **$3,600** over 4 weeks

## Step 3: Gross Cash Comparison (Sales − Rent)
- **Riverside:** $10,080 − $3,600 = **$6,480 profit**
- **Union Station:** $3,600 − $8,000 = **–$4,400 loss** (and it blows the budget outright)
- **Campus library:** $3,600 − $2,400 = **$1,200 profit**

## Step 4: Bags vs. Cups
Riverside's whole-bean bags carry a much higher ticket size ($14) than single cups ($5–$6), and beans have better margins since there's no cup, lid, or sleeve cost eating into each sale. Riverside wins on both volume of profit and quality of margin — Union Station's high foot traffic doesn't make up for its rock-bottom conversion rate and low-margin cup sales, and it can't even fit the budget.

**Recommendation: Riverside Farmers Market**
